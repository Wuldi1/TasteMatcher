# Operations redesign: upload, imports, and management

Source audit at `750d16a9`, 5 October 2026. This is a proposed redesign, not a record of production changes or a live usability test. Source paths below are relative to the repository root. No production data, emails, or uploads were changed.

The design principle is an art business workbench: quiet surfaces, clear responsibility, and a strong distinction between preparing information and committing it. Artwork needs room; administrative records need density. These should share typography, controls, and feedback without sharing one generic page template.

## Common visual language

Use a warm off-white canvas (`#F6F4EF`), white panels, ink text (`#242A25`), muted text (`#666A61`), and a restrained green primary action (`#344D40`). Use separators (`#DEDDD5`) before shadows. These are proposed replacements for the existing `--color-*` tokens, not an additional competing palette. Amber and red identify review needs and errors; role labels remain neutral so role is not confused with health. Verify final token combinations for contrast in implementation.

The page title may use an editorial serif consistent with the wider redesign, but field labels, tables, amounts, and actions stay in the existing sans family. Prefer 14–16px body/input text, 12–13px secondary metadata, tabular numerals for counts/prices, 8px field/button radii and 12px workspace panel radii. Avoid pill-shaped ordinary inputs and three different accent colors for adjacent settings. Use one prominent primary action per stage; secondary actions use ink/border, and destructive controls use red only inside their menu or confirmation.

Labels describe the user's business: “Gallery” is preferable to “Domain” in customer-facing copy while identifiers and API contracts remain unchanged. “Specialist” continues to map to `dealer`. Do not rename statuses into new backend states. Use 44px targets for primary/mobile controls and at least 40px dense desktop controls, with visible focus indicators and meaningful text labels for icon buttons.

## Upload: an artwork preparation workspace

**New structure.** A concise header reads “Add artwork” with the target gallery and an “Import several artworks” secondary link for roles allowed to import. Below, use a 38/62 split: a sticky image stage on the left and four clearly separated form sections on the right. The image stage shows image/file details, replace/remove controls below the art, and a small preview of how the artwork's title/artist/price visibility will read. The form sections are “Artwork details,” “Price & sale,” “Visibility,” and “Description & tags.” A persistent bottom action row repeats the target gallery, displays actionable validation text, and offers “Add artwork.”

The image stage replaces the current giant top-of-form upload area, so the collector-facing image remains visible while details are edited. Do not turn the metadata into a mandatory stepper; experienced users need to complete it in one pass. Optional physical details can fold below title/artist, but validation must open the relevant section.

## Automatic Uploads: a review desk

**New structure.** Preserve the route and workflow, but present the header as “Import artworks” with source modes “Auction URL” and “Import JSON.” The initial view is a focused setup panel with source, supported-provider feedback, and target gallery. The CTA is “Preview lots”; a short sentence says “Nothing is added until you approve.” A JSON hint explicitly says generated import package, not raw PDF.

After preview, replace the large setup form with a compact source strip and “Change source.” The main review desk has a lot list occupying roughly 60% and a details inspector at 40%. A slim toolbar shows source count, selected count, and quick views “All / Needs attention / Ready / Excluded.” These quick views are proposed client-side filters over current draft data; they are not persisted workflow states. Use a simple list/table with thumbnail, lot number, artist/title, estimate, issue status, and an explicit checkbox. Clicking the lot label opens the inspector; it must not toggle inclusion.

The inspector contains the larger image, editable catalog fields, source estimate and conversion explanation, issues linked to their fields, and access controls. Provide previous/next lot buttons and visible lot position. Keep selection, issue filters, and scroll position stable while correcting values. The final sticky bar says “Approve 24 artworks for [gallery]” and explains blockers. A review-only approval summary can show selected scope and flags before the final action, but do not add repeated confirmations to every batch edit.

**Requirements that survive the redesign.** Owners remain fixed to their own gallery; global admins explicitly choose a target. Automatic Uploads remains restricted to `domain_owner`/`global_admin` (`frontend/src/routes/AppRoutes.tsx:155`). Preview and approval are distinct. Changing source/target invalidates preview; warn before losing edited draft work. No persisted drafts, history, schedules, raw PDF upload, or cross-session resume are currently available. Any “Save draft” feature would require a new persistence design and must not appear as working in the prototype.

Approval is disabled if there is no selection or any included blocking draft. Preserve immutable source identity/image provenance, independently editable artwork fields, title/artist/source image requirements, nonnegative dimensions/prices, upper-price bound, required auction date, and tag limits. Keep date inputs local with explicit timezone context, normalize to ISO UTC for approval, and show conversion warnings. Supported URL feedback must reflect the existing exact provider allowlist. For source details that cannot be fetched, show the existing warning without inventing missing metadata.

Requests are processed sequentially in chunks of at most 20; a failed request stops further chunks. The UI may show “40 of 72 processed” only as real chunks complete. Never imply an abort will roll back completed writes. Keep created/skipped results, failed drafts, and untouched drafts separately identifiable. Offer “Retry eligible” only where the current per-item result is retryable; never silently retry writes. A batch warning such as truncation must stay near the source summary even when the operator filters the table.

**Mobile.** Use a list of concise thumbnail rows with checkbox, title, lot number, and status. Detail opens a full-screen editor with “Back to lots”; preserve the queue's position. Bulk selection count and approve bar remain visible. Provide the same editable fields and warning messages as desktop. Do not require horizontal scrolling across a full editable spreadsheet.

## Management: one shell, four distinct workspaces

The persistent management header shows gallery context, scope (“This gallery” or “All galleries”), and current task. Global admins retain four tabs; other authorized staff receive the Users workspace. Gallery context belongs near the title and must remain visible inside every invite/edit/send action. Search is the widest control, filters are compact, and status counts can function as filters when derived from loaded data. Avoid invented growth charts, revenue metrics, or data-health scores.

Global-admin tabs remain “Galleries / People / Gallery requests / Customer requests” in the proposed copy. Distinguish table navigation, row selection, and row actions; repeated row buttons do not all deserve primary emphasis. A persistent action menu must be usable by keyboard and touch, never hover-only. Desktop rows have generous hit targets with compact padding and proper table headings; mobile uses the same data expressed as cards, not a reduced feature set.

**Page structure.** Header with selected gallery and primary “Invite person”; secondary “Customer email.” A search/filter row; a results count; a quiet directory table; optional person detail drawer. Columns: person (name + email), role, status, joined; invited-by and last-update move into the details drawer. All original fields remain inspectable. Name opens details; the action menu contains eligible edit, resend, delete.

| Upload/import/campaign durable outcome                      | Notice is inserted with no shared treatment     | State indication                                  | Once per operation; communicate outcome without delaying recovery                       | Opacity 0 → 1 over 150ms `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`; no sliding numbers, confetti or automatic disappearance; same gentle opacity under reduced motion                                                             |

**Deliberately rejected motion.** Import table rows must not stagger on preview/filter/edit: users read and select them. Search results and combobox keyboard traversal must not animate: high-frequency keyboard interaction. Management tab changes and sorting must not slide tables: frequent navigation and functional data. Artwork crop handles should respond directly to input: no easing lag during manipulation. Campaign preview should not animate on every keystroke: it is live reading material. No animated KPI counters or gradient backgrounds are justified in these operations pages.

Hover transforms are unnecessary here. If implementation adds any, gate them with `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`. Preserve immediate focus indication and high-frequency feedback. Check occasional transitions at 2–5× duration and normal speed, rapid open/close, reduced motion, touch, and keyboard. Source inspection cannot establish the final animation's perceived quality.

## Prototype requirements and validation handoff

Create simulated screens for Upload empty/selected/auction/error/success; Imports setup URL/JSON, populated preview with blocker, bulk edit, lot detail, approval summary and mixed result; Management all four tabs, person and gallery details, invite/create/edit/destructive dialogs, and campaign compose/recipients/review/mixed result. All data and outcomes must be visibly demonstrative. No prototype button may call production upload, delete, invitation, or campaign APIs.

Acceptance scenarios:

1. Switching image does not clear metadata; fixed-price/auction values and unit/currency helper states remain coherent; failure preserves draft; success names the artwork.
2. An owner cannot change target gallery or open global-only management tabs; specialist invitations offer customer role only; protected/self user row actions remain unavailable.
3. Import preview writes nothing. Blocking drafts cannot be approved unless corrected or excluded. Warning-only drafts remain eligible; batch edits affect only selected drafts and only explicitly applied properties.
4. Mixed import results distinguish successful, already imported, failed, and unprocessed items; completed writes stay completed after a later chunk failure; no automatic retry or invented rollback.
5. Every management tab exposes its current information and permitted actions. Gallery Requests remains read-only. Search-empty and data-empty have different copy.
6. Email visible-scope selection stays selected across search, eligibility is preserved, final review repeats exact scope, pending prevents duplicate send, partial result is not called full success; no unsupported retry promise.
7. All overlays can be navigated, closed, and reopened by keyboard with focus return; form labels/errors and status announcements work; all functionality is available at 390px width and 200% zoom with action bars not covering fields.
8. Screens use the same action hierarchy, tokens, statuses and field anatomy; tables remain stable through sort/filter; movement is suppressed appropriately under reduced motion.
